# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

Single self-contained static HTML file (no framework, no build step), hosted on GitHub Pages from this repo. Chosen by the user; must load fast on eSIM data.

## Users

One person: a 29-year-old solo food vlogger from New Zealand, on a six-day trip to Hong Kong and Macau (Sat 28 Nov – Thu 3 Dec 2026). Wakes early, eats a lot, based at an Airbnb in Jordan, Kowloon (Kim Tak Building, 328–342A Nathan Road). Uses the app one-handed on a phone, outdoors, on the move, often while filming.

## Product Purpose

A pocket run-sheet for the vlog: what's next, how to get there, what to order, and what to say on camera. Success: the user never has to stop and think about the next move or the next line to camera.

## Operating Context

- Day picker (Sat 28 – Thu 3) → stops for that day as tappable cards → next/previous between stops.
- Per stop: time, English + Chinese name, a 2–3 sentence casual first-person vlog intro, an "About this place" background block (a few sentences to talk over on camera), 2–4 accurate fun facts, what to order and "While you eat" prompts (food stops: what to point the camera at, and how to describe taste and texture), how to get there, Google Maps link.
- Each stop opens on a Script view (switchable to Info, choice remembered): the stop as numbered beats to follow on camera. Open → Set the scene → What's on the table → First bite → Drop a fact → Verdict → Hand-off to the next stop. Sights get Look around and How it feels instead of the food beats.
- Travel legs (MTR, bus, minibus, tram, ferry, cable car) are smaller, simpler cards between stops.
- A spoken day intro at the start of each day.
- Full-screen prompter with big text for reading intros on camera. Used on the same phone as the front camera, so text sits at the top of the screen near the lens.
- Auto-highlights the current/next stop using Hong Kong time (UTC+8) during the trip.
- "Mark visited" per stop, stored on the device.
- Cantonese phrases tab: m goi, do je, ho sik, mai dan, gei do chin, ni go, yat go, m sai, jo san, dong naai cha, siu laat / dai laat, with characters and meaning.
- Used in bright daylight, in queues, on trams and ferries, and at night markets.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Phone first, big tap targets, one-handed use.
- Light and dark mode.
- Fun facts must be accurate; anything uncertain is left out.
- Itinerary content is the user's, as given in the brief; times are plans, not guarantees.

## Brand Commitments

No channel name, catchphrase or sign-off. Intros stay natural, casual first person. Voice: food-documentary storytelling. Scene-setting and dry humour with respect for the people doing the work (Bourdain-like) on camera; how it's made, why it tastes that way and "think of it as…" comparisons (food-vlogger-like) in the background and eating prompts. Colour never replaces facts.

## Product Principles

1. Next move first: the current or next stop is always one glance away.
2. Say it, then go: the camera line and the directions are the two things every stop must deliver.
3. Truth over colour: no invented facts, prices or opening hours.
4. Readable in the street: legible in sun, at arm's length, one thumb.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Large type and high contrast for outdoor reading; tap targets of at least 48px; respects reduced motion.
