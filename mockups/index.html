Light mode, all three showing the same Sunday at 08:12 (a sample time). Each row is three phone screens: the day's stops, one stop opened, and the prompter. Tell me which look you want, or what to swap between them. Swipe sideways on a phone to see all three screens.

A · Tram rollsign

Assigned direction

Built from the Ding Ding trams: deep tram green for the app's frame, and the black bilingual destination sign at the top always showing your next stop. Tapping Next flips the sign over like a tram blind. Stops are white tickets with big check buttons; travel legs are dotted lines between them.

About this place

Sham Shui Po is one of Hong Kong's oldest working-class districts: fabric wholesalers, electronics stalls on Apliu Street, and some of the cheapest good food in the city. Hop Yik Tai is a tiny takeaway counter best known for cheung fun, steamed rice-noodle rolls drowned in sweet, peanut and sesame sauces. Across the road, Kung Wo is an old-style beancurd shop that makes its own tofu, soy milk and tofu pudding.

Order

Cheung fun, all sauces腸粉Tofu pudding豆腐花Soy milk豆漿

Fun facts

  • Cheung fun means "intestine noodle", named for the rolled shape.
  • Hop Yik Tai is listed in the Michelin Guide Hong Kong.

B · MTR line map

Impeccable's pick

Each day is drawn as an MTR line in its own line colour (Sunday is Tsuen Wan red), with stops as stations and travel legs as labelled stretches of track. An opened stop gets an MTR-style station-colour panel with a white name plate, Chinese name on top. Risk: this is the obvious Hong Kong reference, so it's the most familiar of the three.
